# Constellation · how it works

Three things live here, on one deployment:

| URL | What it is |
|---|---|
| `/constellation` | The prototype: the member app and the coach console, with the demo clock along the top |
| `/constellation/library` | The Library: every story and every life in the corpus, to read and to wander |
| `/api/constellation` | The back end: members, coaches, the corpus, the matcher, and the record of source checks |

All three are `noindex`. The data is sample data: members and companies are fictional, heroes are real people.

## The prototype, as exported

The prototype is a Claude Design export, unbundled by `tools/unbundle.py`. Its files stay as exported:

- `index.html` is a template (`<x-dc>`) that `js/support.js` (the design runtime) compiles to React. `{{ }}` binds a value, `sc-if` shows a block, `sc-for` repeats one.
- `js/constellation-proto-data.js` sets `window.CPD`: 107 heroes with 13 sample stories, the six members and three coaches, the messages, and the demo clock.
- `js/constellation-proto-engine.js` sets `window.CPE`. `init()` builds the demo state; `vals()` turns that state into every value the template binds; `mount()` wires resize, keys and the persona.

The engine runs the programme's rules:

- **The bracket.** 16 contenders: 8 leaders (cross-generational first, then nearest the member's formative years, born + 10 to born + 25) and 8 from interests (8, 4 + 4 or 3 + 3 + 2). Pairings keep finalists apart. The member keeps three; the fourth is the reserve.
- **The fit check.** Each of the three lines up, partly fits, or pulls the other way. A coach can keep, scope or swap a hero that pulls.
- **The selection rule.** A story must carry the week's situation and be verified. Then it comes from the member's three before their extended heroes before the library. Far stories go to Ask answers only. No hero twice in 14 days, nothing the member declined, and a tie breaks on id.
- **Precedence.** Pinned beats paired in Moves, which beats approved or swapped in Review, which beats the rule. Review auto-sends at 18:00 on Sunday unless a member is on Hold.
- **The move.** It passes at least five of the six tests, and the behaviour runs to 18 words or fewer.

The demo clock steps through days 6 to 21: the bracket, final four and fit check, the coach's roster, publishing Move 1, the Monday card, the run sheet, the debrief, an Ask, the review queue and check-in prep.

## How the corpus reaches it

```
constellation-corpus (data/)                    calidescope (constellation/)
  scripts/export_app.py  ───────────────▶  data/corpus.json      437 lives, 525 stories, 22 situations,
                                                                 66 moves, 66 lines, calibration, outcomes
  js/constellation-proto-data.js ─ tools/build_demo.mjs ─▶ data/demo.json
                                                                 members, coaches, messages, heroes, works,
                                                                 the 13 samples, the id map, the twins,
                                                                 labels for all 22 situations
                                          tools/build_demo.mjs ─▶ data/derived.json
                                                                 each story's counted words, how often each
                                                                 life answers a week, the corpus's SHA-256
                     lib/core.js  merge(corpus, demo)  ─▶  one index: 531 stories from 476 lives
```

- **What the export leaves behind.** Survey popularity numbers and flags stay out, and so do law references and titles; both need a licence before they reach a product. Quote texts and candidates stay out too. The export refuses to write if any of them slips through.
- **The id map.** 68 of the prototype's 107 heroes are on the roster (`obama` is `barack-obama`). The other 39 join the Library under a slug of their name, with no story yet.
- **Twins.** Seven samples tell the same moment as a corpus record, such as the Law Review election. Each sample keeps its id, because the demo script points at it, and takes the corpus record's keys and examples. The corpus copy is not listed twice, and its id resolves to the sample everywhere.
- **Situations.** The prototype listed 11 situations with a label and a line. All 22 now have one: five lines and six labels are new, written to match. A1 takes the corpus key, `unnamed-expectations`.
- **The bridge** (`js/constellation-bridge.js`) runs from the page head. It holds the engine back until the corpus and the demo world have loaded, then merges them into `window.CPD`. The back end's demo world (`/api/constellation/demo`, else `data/demo.json`) and the ledger of source checks are waited for 1.5 s at most; a ledger that comes later is applied when it lands, so a slow or hung `/state` never holds the corpus back. The runtime renders twice, so it feeds and wraps again on every assignment. The engine's rules are untouched. Corpus stories arrive as recall drafts, so the rule still sends verified stories only, and the scripted demo plays out as designed. A coach's source check in the Library lifts a draft at once.
- **The template patches** (`tools/patches.py`: 93 on the template and 20 on the engine, each listed at the top of the file) add the Library card on Heroes, the Library buttons on a hero page and a story, the Library overlay, the coach's Explore tab, related reading under a story and a hero, "How it reaches your three", "Who stands near your three", "Find a story for this" under a member's Ask, While you wait on This week, the 18:00 manifest on Review, the first-week block at Publish Move 1, and the demo bar at every width. Two passes follow them: the type floor, and curly apostrophes, which set a straight apostrophe inside a word curly in the template's text (never an attribute or a binding) and in the engine's strings ("I’m ready"). `unbundle.py` re-applies them to every new export and refuses if an anchor has moved. Anchors are written as the export writes them, so they match a fresh export and the floored page alike, and a fresh export reproduces today's `index.html`.
- **Three engines, one index.** `lib/core.js` (the merge, the matcher, likeness, one title and one year per story), `lib/ask.js` (a question read into a query and answered, the shelf of coach questions, compare, gaps) and `lib/net.js` (kindred ties with their reasons, routes between two lives, how a story reaches a member's three, where the three stand, families of moves, and what next for a member). All are pure and run in the browser, the app and the API alike. What next has one ranker, `CLN.fitFor`, and one answer per member and week, `CLN.nextFor`, which the member's While you wait and Next, the coach's line, Details, the Ask reply and the plan all read. A count asks for numbers alone: `CLQ.answer(lib, q, { count: true })` returns `{ total, lives }` before any ranking or card, and the shelf, the gaps and a story's "a quieter way" count with it. `CLN.bridge` keeps each chain it finds by member, life, week and three, and a check starts them afresh.
- **Tables that ship.** `data/derived.json` holds what a page would otherwise build on its first story: each story's counted words (about 125 KB) and how often each life answers a week. It carries the SHA-256 of `corpus.json`'s bytes, worked out off the main thread, and a basis: a fingerprint of the code that builds the tables, the field of every life and the demo's own stories. A page reads the tables only when both match, and builds them as before when either does not, so a new corpus or a changed rule never meets stale tables. Families of moves are built in the page, so a change to how moves meet needs no rebuild. What does not ship is built while the page is idle (`CLC.warm`), in slices of 30 ms at most that give a tap the main thread back (`CLN.idleRun`): the move vectors, the text vectors, then every situation's families, the open member's own first. In the app the chains from the member's three to the lives in their weeks follow, then each member's season and weeks as the Review asks for them. A first story, a first situation page and the first Review find them made, and so does the Library's frame, which reads the app's index. `CLC.sentences`, `teaser` and `lead` are kept by text or story id, `CLC.sourceFor` by source, reader and status, `CLQ.gaps` while the checks and each member's heroes, week and age hold, and the Review's plan until a check is made or taken back.
- **One Library for the session.** Inside the app the Library reads the app's own index (`parent.CLB.lib`) and fetches no data, the coach's Explore included. Its frame is mounted at the first open and kept, hidden between opens and routed by `cl:go`; a fresh open starts its own Back afresh, it comes back scrolled where the reader left it, and saves and checks made while it was hidden reach it when it is shown. After 50 pages it loads afresh at the next open, so nobody is thrown out mid-read. A response that arrives after its page has moved on is dropped (`getJSON` takes a token and an AbortController), so one member's late season never paints on another's page. On its own the Library waits 1.5 s at most for the shared ledger, opens on this browser's checks, and applies the ledger when it lands.

## The back end · `/api/constellation`

One function, `api/constellation.js`. `vercel.json` rewrites `/api/constellation/<path>` to it.

| Route | Answers with |
|---|---|
| `GET /` | counts and the route list |
| `GET /members`, `/members/:id` | the six members, with situations, heroes and coach |
| `GET /members/:id/reading` | a coach's prep reading: stories for their situations, from their heroes first |
| `GET /members/:id/season?weeks=8` | their weeks as the corpus matcher would serve them |
| `GET /coaches`, `/coaches/:id` | Jim, Bret and Jennifer, and who each coaches |
| `GET /demo` | the demo world the prototype boots from |
| `GET /icons`, `/icons/:id` | lives, with filters (`field`, `domain`, `stance`, `sit`, `era`, `q`), stories, works and trails |
| `GET /stories`, `/stories/:id` | stories, with filters (`sit`, `icon`, `reach`, `status`, `era`) and trails |
| `GET /situations`, `/situations/:id` | the 22 situations, with moves, lines, calibration and outcome |
| `GET /eras`, `/eras/:id`, `/search?q=` | eras and search |
| `GET /stories/:id/similar` | the same kind of week, and the same world, each with its reason |
| `GET /icons/:id/kindred`, `/icons/:id/ego` | kin (a shared situation and one more reason, said in words), and the neighbourhood to draw |
| `GET /situations/web` | the situations, joined where the same lives lived both |
| `GET /path?from=&to=` | up to three ways from one life to another, every step with its reasons |
| `GET /ask?t=&m=` | a question read into a query (chips, other readings, words not used) and answered with reasons; or pass the query itself: `sit`, `sex`, `field`, `before`, `after`, `age`, `dir`, `reach`, `ok`, `m` |
| `GET /members/:id/shelf` | the questions coaches bring, for this member, each counted |
| `GET /members/:id/near` | where the member's three stand among the member's situations, the lives in more than one, stand-ins |
| `GET /state?ns=` | the shared ledger of source checks, and this browser's own record |
| `POST /state/ledger` | `{ story, by, note }`: a coach has read a story against its source |
| `DELETE /state/ledger?story=` | take a check back |
| `PUT /state/user?ns=` | `{ saved, trails }`: this browser's own record |

The matcher in `lib/core.js` is a line-for-line port of the corpus repo's `scripts/match.py`. The test suite replays all 15 member files for 40 weeks through both and compares every pick: 600 member-weeks, identical.

**Storage.** With a Redis store connected, the ledger and saved trails persist and are shared between coaches. Add the Upstash Redis integration to the Vercel project: it sets `KV_REST_API_URL` and `KV_REST_API_TOKEN`, and the code reads them, or `UPSTASH_REDIS_REST_URL` and `UPSTASH_REDIS_REST_TOKEN`. Without a store, records sit in the function's memory, which Vercel recycles. Each browser then keeps its own copy, so the demo works with or without one.

**Writes are open.** Anyone with the URL can mark a story checked. That suits a demo. Put sign-in in front before working coaches rely on it.

## The Library · `/constellation/library`

- **The constellation.** Each star is a life, placed by field and by the year of its defining moment; larger stars hold more stories. Pick one of the 22 situations and its lives light up and join in time order. Hover a star and threads run to its closest kin, across fields and centuries; a thread that would leave the top of the map bows downward. Tap a star once on a phone, or reach it by keyboard (the map is one tab stop: left and right move through a field in time, up and down change field, Enter opens), and the twelve nearest lives are listed under the map, each with its year and the situation they share. On a phone the map opens on the last century, its field names held still at the left edge as it scrolls, and a tap on a field's band names the lives of that field in view.
- **Pages.** A life (stories, defining moments, works, kindred across time); a story (source, caution, status; others who lived this week, each with the reason that sets it apart; every story of the week plotted by year and field; the other way, a life that met the same week by the opposite means); a situation (a path in five stories, from the plainest case to the furthest, and every story of the week); an era, a field, a stance, people, search.
- **Ask.** A question in plain words ("She is announcing the reorg on Thursday, and the person who wanted her job will be in the room") is read into chips: the situations, a woman or a man, a field, an era, an age, a quieter register, the member it is for. Every chip can be taken away, the situation can be changed, other readings are offered and words that were not used are named. A question can anchor on a life: "a quieter story than Obama" keeps Obama's situations and finds who met them less directly than Obama did, each card saying so ("Measured, where Obama was direct"); a story met head on links to the same week, a quieter way. It also reads who the line will be said to ("the colleague who wanted the job", "a client or their counsel") and the room ("a team meeting", "a pitch"); both rank and never filter, and each card then shows the line and the move set in that room. A room the announcement is placed in comes before the team an announcement implies ("announces the reorg in an email" is in writing, "at this week's board meeting" the board), and a room only mentioned leaves it the team's ("announces the committee's decision to her team"). A line said to a named person is a one-to-one ("What should Sarah tell Dan"); a department, a group or the question's own member is no such person ("said to Marketing", "tell Head Office", "tell Dan's team", "What should I tell Sarah"), and another member is ("What can Maya say to Tom"). The question is for the member it is about: one named only as the one spoken to, for an age ("Michael's age") or in a clause that sets the scene ("When Sarah announces the reorg, what should Tom tell his team?") leaves it to the one who acts. A line is asked for in words for it: "Sarah can tell her team is anxious" is seeing it, "How can Sarah tell whether the team trusts her?" is judging it, and "Can you tell me a story where…" asks for a story. Turning something down is a choice when the member, I or we did it ("Priya turned them down"), or the one offered it did ("She was offered the role and turned it down"); the setback is the member turned down by somebody else ("the VP turned her down"), and once the member has pitched or asked someone, the she or he who turns it down is that person ("Maya pitched the roadmap to the VP and she turned it down"). A loss is somebody else's only when their name opens the sentence ("Richard Nixon narrowly lost"); the loss of the person's own work stays theirs. A board or committee asked to back a plan adds the coalition and leads only when nothing plainer is said ("Michael needs the board to approve his pitch" is the pitch, then the board). A member named keeps the answer inside their own situations, a scene asked for on its own ("who got fired") is the question, and words that name no situation are said so, with the closest three. For a coach the answer leads with two cards: a checked story ready to send, leaving out what the member already has, and the draft one check from sending, with its source: its first clause, never cut at an abbreviation ("the Rev. James Drake") and with no working note left in it ("page to be confirmed"), and for a citation still being checked, the source the record names for the moment beside it ("Michelle Obama, Becoming (Crown, 2018); the moment is from her speech to the Democratic National Convention…"). Each card's route names the member's hero by the short name, or by the whole one when a life in the line holds it too ("one step from Barack Obama" on Michelle Obama's card); a member page's first three keep one place for what the member had, a longer list two. What the Ask reads into a story stays beside it, so the API sends the story as the corpus has it. On a phone the reading folds to one line. The answer counts its stories and lives, offers ways to narrow with their counts, gives each card the reasons that set it apart, and when fewer than four stories fit, says what to drop. The query is the address, so an answer is a link.
- **The shelf** (coach). The questions coaches bring, grouped (what is happening, who my member is, who will land with them, how it should land), each with its live count, the member's own week first. **Where the answers are thin** lists the questions with fewer than four stories and a grid of the situations against what coaches ask for. **Compare** sets two stories side by side: what they share, then only the rows where they differ.
- **Families of moves** (a situation page): the situation's stories joined where one clause of each makes the same move (`CLN.coLocated`). Two of the same words must sit side by side in each clause, within four words and in the same order, one of them outside the week's commonest; or anywhere in the clause when neither is among the commonest and one is rare. Two clauses that share only the frame of a move share no move: the verbs that open almost every move and the people and things they are done to ("ask your manager", "tell your team", "chair", "organisation") need a word of the move itself beside them, in the clause's own head, and a clause that does its work on a bare "it" or "them" stands with nobody. The words cannot tell a word's two senses apart ("each senior colleague" and "the senior seat", "the one thing you learned" and "the one thing you will work on"), so round six read by hand every tie that holds a family and every same move a page says, 222 of them, and keeps apart the 53 pairs that make two different moves (`APART` in `lib/net.js`); a new corpus wants its ties read the same way. A clause is one whole action: a fragment, a piece with no verb of its own ("the direction you want", "naming the review you skipped", "so the numbers are read beside it") and whatever follows a clause too short to stand ("speak once, second to last, …") read with the clause before it, and a condition takes the clause it governs ("if it could sit on a rival's slide, rewrite it there and then"). A row shows the clause its tie is on, and its shared words are marked in any form ("repeat", "repeated"). Words weigh against the situation's own stories, a pivot word reads with the word beside it ("one sentence", "say it first"), and a reason says only the words both stories wrote in the same form. A story joins a family through its hub's clause, or through two members that make the move as it does. Each family is a card headed by the clause most of its stories make ("Write the one sentence you want repeated in the corridor afterwards"), its caption in words ("Four stories, all recall drafts"); each row gives a short name and the year, then the row's own clause at 16px with the shared words marked, and the card is a plain list of links, with arcs in the gutter for the ties that hold it. The three largest families are open and the rest fold under "Two more families, and 29 moves of their own"; the arcs repaint when the fold opens. E1 has five families holding 12 of its 41 stories, and its moves fit in 882px on a phone. Across the corpus, 67 families hold 175 of 563 story slots, with 154 ties. An Others card whose story makes the same move gives both clauses, so a reader can see it is one move ("The same move. Pat Benatar: say once, at your normal volume, the direction you want and the reason, then stop. James Madison: answer its two strongest claims in order, at your normal volume, and stop."). A member's situation page leads with the stories closest to their three, from one of the three, then a step from one, then two, each saying so ("One step from Obama", and "One step from Barack Obama" on Michelle Obama's card, as the Ask's route says it), and the path in five, the families and the map wait behind "More ways to read this week". A story page adds "The same move in another week" when the nearest move in another situation shares a clause with it, and gives both clauses ("Motley: tell the partner leading it which part you will present." and "Beers: ask the partner leading it for one section you will present alone, and present it."). Of 531 stories, 12 have one; David Ogilvy's D3 story finds Steve Jobs's E3, and neither Katherine Johnson (to Bezos, on "manager" and "meeting") nor Jobs (to Murakami, on "tell one colleague") meets anyone on chance words. A member reads the other week in quotation marks ("Charlotte Beers, 1970, “Selling what you used to make”"). The life map waits behind "See the lives on the map", and the 3x3 grid is the coach's.
- **Networks.** Kindred across time on each life: a list with every tie said in words, and, when a tie crosses more than fifty years, a band for each situation the life shares with its kin, years across. **Situations that travel together**: the 22 as rows in words, each saying how many lives hold it and the two situations it travels with most ("A1 · Nobody told you what the job asks · 30 lives · most often with C3 (4) and E1 (3)"); a row opens on who lived both. **Connect two lives**: the strongest way and up to two others, each step a shared situation and one more reason. **Who stands near your three**: the member's situations as overlapping circles, their three where they stand, the lives in more than one, and for each hero the nearest life where they have no story; a reason given once is pointed to after that ("Nearest to Obama: Billie Jean King, for the reasons given above."). **At your age**: every story in the member's situations placed by the age its person was then, beside the member's own age, the nearest named. A way of leading is a reading until a coach has ruled on it for both lives: it is never the reason a tie stands on (ties go from 3,715 to 2,781, and Aristotle and Beethoven, whose only second reason it was, have no kin), it weighs 1 in a nearness, and it comes last among the reasons, said as a reading ("Both may stay close to their people. A coach has yet to rule on this."). No member card leads with one. "Close to" and "Nearest to" rest only on what the record holds: a situation, the field, or years within 15.
- **Trails.** Every page offers ways on: the same situation in another century, contemporaries, the same field, the same way of leading. Every page opened joins the trail along the top, and a trail can be kept and walked again.
- **Modes.**
  - Reader: the default when standalone.
  - Member: inside the app, starting from the member's three.
  - Coach: everything, plus moves, lines, calibration, prep for each member, the season ahead, and source checks. Source checks (`#/checks`) lists what members asked for first, then each member's first check for the week, the console coach's own members first ("· Bret’s member"), from `CLN.nextFor` as their Review line does ("Ready to send without a check: …" where a checked story carries the week), then **Needs a source**, the three drafts whose source names no work to check against (Ali 1967, Ali at Miami Beach 1964 and the Gloucester Hotel 1973; a dated address, speech or statement counts as a work; `CLN.sourceOk`, and no fill, ready pick or plan offers them), and **Years to settle**, the 12 stories whose dateline gave a year the record does not ("Years disagree: 1953, late 1950s."), a note each of their check cards carries too.
- **Inside the app, both ways (member).**
  - A story ends with three: the next story, when the member wants it (ranked by `CLN.fitFor`, as While you wait is, with the open story's week first), then two others who lived the same week, the nearest the three first, each with its own reason ("One step from Obama: both had every act read as a signal"), and one way into the library: "All of this week, in the library". Each opens in place, with Back walking the stack. A story from beyond the three says how it reaches them ("Elena Kagan is one step from Obama, one of your three"), each person at the year of their own story in the situation they share. A hero page lists all their stories and kindred lives, each tie said in words, a reason said once and pointed to after ("As above, and two years apart.").
  - The reader keeps their place: a story opened from another starts at its top, Back returns each page to where it was left, and Back from "Ask Jim about this" in the Library returns to the Library page it came from. In the Library a member reads situations in words, with no codes (the ladder's "Open A1" reads "Open: Nobody told you what the job asks").
  - While you wait. A held week shows one row under "Your story comes once Bret has checked it.": WHILE YOU WAIT, then "Michelle Obama · Philadelphia, July 2016", then "Checked. From Michelle Obama, your reserve, for another of your weeks: everything you do gets read as a signal." ("for this week: …" only for the week it is). It is the member's best fit by `CLN.fitFor`: 8 for a checked story, 4 for one of the three, 3 for the reserve or an extended hero, 2 for one link to the three and 1 for two, 1.5 for a field among their interests, and 1 for an age within four of theirs where the year holds, leaving out what they had, declined or have read, the held story and any life from the last 14 days, so once read it moves on, as Next does. The reason is said from the strongest term, and a draft's opens "Recall draft. Bret checks it before it is sent." A tap opens it in place, and Back returns to This week at the same scroll.
  - A member's story is honest once: a draft carries one mark, "Recall draft", with a 44px info button beside it whose line names the work the story's citation gives ("Written from memory. Bret reads it against the source, the NASA oral-history interviews, before it is sent, so every detail you repeat holds."; "its source" where the citation names none). The line is open on the first draft a member meets and folded after. A draft shows no coach's working note or internal id, and a translation line only where the note names a published one ("Translation: Jack Flam, Matisse on Art (revised edition, 1995)"); a checked story shows its source and one sentence of context. "Why it fits" names the week ("It fits your week: …" for this week's, "It fits one of your weeks: …" for another), and a story page reads one week throughout: Why, Others who lived this week and All of this week all take the member's own week the story carries.
  - One year per story. The record's year gives the dateline, the chip and the title where the dateline agrees; where its year words disagree, the dateline keeps its place and no year at all until a coach settles it (Katherine Johnson's "late 1950s" reads "Langley, Virginia", her title "Katherine Johnson, Langley"), no other year is reckoned from it ("Also in science, 1952", never "one year earlier"), and no body opens with its own dateline. Under the title, a story the coach chose for the week says so ("Bret chose this for Thursday’s product review."). The record holds a year of birth, so an age line says the two ages it could be ("Billie Jean King was 29 or 30 then, about two years younger than you are."), only on a checked story whose years agree; "How it reaches your three" is a closed disclosure below, with a caret that turns, shown within two steps.
  - The Library opens under one bar: Back, named for where it goes ("‹ Kagan"), the page's title ("Others who lived Kagan's week") and Browse, which opens the library's front page with its search. Back walks the pages read in the frame one at a time, to the place on each page where the reader was, then returns to the story, the hero or the tab it was opened from. Inside the app the frame adds no bars of its own. Save there says "Saved. It's on You."
  - The trail comes home. You shows "Your last trail" with its last three stops (a step back takes a stop off), and This week, below the line, offers to pick up there. This week holds the story, the move and the line. The move's headline is the whole behaviour ("Send one paragraph with a position in it to one person, the day before the meeting."), then this week's step of it ("Week 1 of 6: send it to one person."), with "Six weeks to make this a habit." in week 1 alone; Why opens on the first failure only. Prep names its room and shows the day it opens until it does ("Prep for Thursday’s product review opens Wednesday."), and the line is "Developed with" the member's own coach. An hour before the room the notification names what is ready, with no pronoun read from a name ("In an hour: your move and your line.", and "…your move, your line and Katherine Johnson’s story." once her story is checked).
  - Heroes says what the library holds for each of the three, one status a card: checked stories, and drafts waiting for the coach's check ("1 story in the library, waiting for Bret’s check."). The fit check's line comes from the stories a hero has ("Michelle Obama has a checked story for one of your weeks: everything you do gets read as a signal."; a hero with only a draft adds "It is a recall draft, waiting for Bret’s check."), after the export's note on the hero's record ("Her record is mostly about owning the stage. Serena Williams’s one story is about another kind of week."; "Michael Jordan’s two stories are about other kinds of week."), and a hero nobody in the library is tied to says so, on Heroes and on M4 ("No story from MrBeast yet. Nobody in the library is tied to MrBeast, so Bret chooses your stories by hand."). M2 gives the bracket by its own counts ("Your bracket: 16 people. Eight leaders, and eight from Creators, Sport and Film & TV."). "Who stands near your three" lives on Heroes; You has the last trail, "At your age" and the whole library.
  - On a recall draft, "Ask Jim to check it" puts the story in the coach's thread and a signal on the member's record. The button then reads "Asked. Jim has it." over "You will see it on This week once Jim has checked it.", and You shows "Waiting for Jim's check"; once checked, This week says so. The Ask answer carries the same mark, button and line.
  - Ask lists the member's own situations in their own words, ordered by what they wrote, with "None of these" and "Send it to Jim as it is". The answer leaves out what the member already has and says so ("You already have Barack Obama's story from Chicago, December 2008 for this. ## Refreshing

- **New corpus.** In the corpus repo, run `python3 scripts/export_app.py <calidescope>/constellation/data/corpus.json --parity <calidescope>/constellation/tests/fixtures/parity.json`. Then run `node constellation/tools/build_demo.mjs`, which writes `data/demo.json` and `data/derived.json`, then the tests and both gates.
- **A changed likeness rule.** Run `node constellation/tools/build_demo.mjs` again whenever `likeThis`, the text index, `words`, the stop words, the field of a life or the samples change. Until then the page builds the tables itself, and the core test fails with that command.
- **New prototype export.** Run `python3 -I constellation/tools/unbundle.py <export.html> <phosphor-package-dir>`, which re-applies the patches. Then run `node constellation/tools/build_demo.mjs` and the tests.
