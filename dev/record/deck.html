The outcomes market

Everyone sells “Outcomes.”
How far they stand behind it is on the page.

A page-by-page read of who sells outcomes, how far each one commits, and the language they use to do it.

138 companies read page by page · September 2026 · Calidescope LLC

The story1/17

The summary

The word is the same. The distance behind it is not.

138 advertising and media companies were read page by page. 740 of their claims are about an “Outcome.” What separates them is not what the word means. It is how far each seller is willing to stand behind it, and that is printed on the page.

Why it matters

Two sellers can use the same sentence and stand very different distances behind it. The distance is printed on the page.

By the numbers

740 “Outcome” claims · 290 name the advertiser · 42 show the method · 28 cite an outsider · 8 put something at risk

The surprise

The 20 companies whose product is the outcome show their method twice as often as everyone else, and reach for a bare number less often.

What to watch

Which rung a seller is standing on. It is readable in about a minute, and it does not require trusting anyone.

The story10/17

The precedent

This industry already knows how to put money on a number.

1952–1967

Audience

Radio first, then network television. Mutual’s 1956 guarantee used “make-good time rather than rebates.” By 1985, guaranteed buys were 85% of prime-time sales.

RemedyMake-good time

1995

Impressions

CNET sells the first banner ads with guaranteed audience delivery. The IAB’s Rich LeFurgy: CPM guarantees “made make-goods possible, just like in television.”

RemedyMake-good impressions

2013–2015

Viewability

Google’s viewable CPM, the MRC lifting its advisory, the ANA telling members to pay only for viewable ads. Buyers drove this one, not sellers.

RemedyNo payment

2026

Attention

Vevo in January, Duolingo in September, each guaranteeing a minimum attention score. Neither announcement states a remedy.

RemedyNot stated

The pattern is not nerve. The guarantee attaches once an independent accredited measurer exists: Nielsen, then ratings. The MRC, then viewability. Adelaide, then attention. An outcome has no accredited measurer yet, and that is the better explanation of where the market has stopped.

Each one narrow and conditioned. Meredith went four years without having to honour its guarantee, which its critics read as a bar set where you cannot lose. The rung has been climbed. It has not been settled.

The story14/17

The opportunity

Two moves, depending on where you stand.

If you sell outcomes today

Climb one rung

42 of 740 claims show how the number was measured. Adding the method to a figure you already publish moves you past 95% of the pages in this study.

If you already show your method

Name what you owe

Eight claims in 2,213 put something at risk, and every one names something upstream of the sale. Seven of the twenty outcome sellers already read results against a control.

Neither move needs new evidence. Both are available from what these companies already publish.

The story15/17

The evidence · limits

Five things the second pass stopped us saying.

01

“The market has no proof.”

It has plenty. The question is what each number shows.

02

“Vague language means weak evidence.”

Walled gardens use the plainest language of anyone and show a method on none of their claims.

03

“Outcome sellers are more honest.”

They show their method more often. Honesty was not measured.

04

“No seller puts a fee at risk.”

Sellers have guaranteed a business result with a stated remedy at least five times since 2011.

05

“The gap is the opportunity.”

An absence on a web page is not proof of demand.
